# Elist Analysis

Elist is an e-commerce company that has sold electronics since 2019. I’m partnering with the Head of Operations to analyze the company’s performance from 2019 through 2022, focusing on sales trends, monthly and annual growth rates, loyalty program performance, refund rates, and average order value. Leadership will use the findings to guide business decisions, and the product and marketing teams will use the analysis and recommendations to grow sales and improve the performance of Elist’s e-commerce platform.


# Deep- Dive Insights
## Sales Trends

Sales rose sharply in 2020, with March revenue increasing 50% month over month around the onset of COVID-19. Monthly revenue peaked at $1.25M in December 2020. Sales then weakened, reaching $4.96M in 2022, still 28% above 2019. February 2022 revenue fell 33% and October fell 55% month over month. AOV rose about 13% in February 2020 and fell 18% in October 2022.

## Seaosonality
September and November-December generally show stronger sales; February and October tend to be weaker. Late 2022 was particularly weak compared with earlier holiday periods, although sales did rise from October to December. The May–June and July–September observations suggest possible seasonal opportunities; holiday promotions and back-to-school demand remain explanations to investigate.

## Geography
North America generated approximately $14.3M, more than the other regions combined, while LATAM contributed approximately $1.67M. North America and EMEA together accounted for around 80% of sales, led by the US and Great Britain.

## Products
The 24" Gaming Monitor, AirPods and MacBook Air generated 84.96% of revenue. The  24" Gaming Monitor, AirPods and charging cables generated 86.7% of orders. The monitor and AirPods are therefore important to both revenue and volume. Bose headphones generated under $10K and iPhones approximately $213K. Investigate demand and profitability before changing these offerings.

## Monthly and Yearly Growth
Revenue grew 163% in 2020, alongside 101% order growth and 31% AOV growth. March orders rose from 1,645 to 2,405 (+46% month over month), while revenue rose 50%. Order growth and revenue growth therefore increased at different rates even though they show similar trends.

In 2021, orders reached 35,858 (+6%), but revenue fell 10% because AOV declined 15%. Product mix helps explain this pattern: purchases shifted toward cheaper cables and webcams while orders for higher-priced laptops declined. In 2022, revenue fell 46%, orders decreased 40%, and AOV fell 10%; fewer orders were the larger contributor to that year's decline.

January 2021 orders increased 98% versus January 2020, partly reflecting the lower comparison base. AirPods, monitors and charging cables were leading purchases. After March 2021, year-over-year order growth generally weakened before broad declines in 2022.

Product differences: In 2021, AirPods orders declined about 5%, monitors rose 4%, and webcams rose 132%. Charging cable orders also increased. Their AOV fell from about $22.40 in 2020 to $18.31 in 2021. Bose AOV fell from $123.35 in 2021 to $101.98 in 2022, when only one order was recorded. Lower prices alone did not ensure higher demand; promotions are a hypothesis, not an established cause.

## Loyalty Program Performance

Non-members generated $17.14M across 2019–2022, compared with $10.96M from members. Annual results reveal a reversal: members generated $4.86M versus $4.26M for non-members in 2021, and $2.72M versus $2.24M in 2022. Both groups declined in 2022, so the stronger member position should not be interpreted as uninterrupted revenue growth.

<img width="582" height="353" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/99f44885-210a-4c22-9730-da3bfc31f927" />

AOV also shifted. Member AOV increased from $208.83 in 2019 to $244.99 in 2022. Non-member AOV peaked at $345.37 in 2020 and fell to $214.29 in 2022. Members spent approximately $31 more per order in 2022, reversing the earlier non-member advantage.

Regional differences: In 2020, North America generated $3.75M from non-members and $1.56M from members. Non-member APAC AOV grew approximately 68% that year. LATAM non-member AOV declined approximately 46% in 2021. In 2022, LATAM member AOV declined approximately 21%, highlighting a region requiring closer review.

Recommendation: Continue the program while assessing whether existing non-members became members and remained active. Compare repeat purchases, member and non-member order counts, and program costs. The recent member advantage is encouraging, but the comparison alone does not establish that membership caused higher spending or retention.

## Apple Product Refunds and AOV

North America is the main focus of the refund analysis because it represents approximately 55% of Apple purchases. For North America in 2019–2021, MacBook refund rates were 14.78%, iPhone 11.61%, and AirPods 7.24%. The rates reflect the region and period examined. AirPods had the most refunds, including 865 in North America in 2020, alongside higher order volume.

<img width="583" height="385" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/86f4db6b-4816-41a3-b870-f63740454da7" />

Across all regions, MacBook refund rates fell from 18.31% in 2019 to 16.86% in 2020 and 6.33% in 2021. Thus, the highest refund count in 2020 did not mean the highest refund rate. No refunds were recorded in 2022; confirm completeness before treating this as improved performance.

Refunded orders generally had higher AOV than non-refunded orders, with exceptions for iPhones in 2019–2020. MacBooks had the highest order values. Globally, refunded MacBook orders averaged $1,711.92 in 2021.

Within North America, the three product refund rates averaged 11.21%, with a standard deviation of 3.09 percentage points. MacBook was slightly above the mean-plus-one-standard-deviation level of 14.3

Refunded MacBook price variability in North America increased from roughly $110 in 2019 to $300 in 2021, while non-refunded variability stayed near $264–$287. Investigate refund reasons and price ranges to identify affected purchases.

## Recommendations
1. **Investigate the October decline in order volume.** Identify what drove the drop in October and why it was particularly sharp in 2022.
2. **Review underperforming products.** Work with the Product and Marketing teams to assess whether to remove the Bose headphones from Elist’s assortment given their low sales. Investigate why iPhone sales are weak on Elist despite the product’s broader popularity.
3. **Continue the loyalty program while evaluating its impact.** Keep the program in place while collecting more data to determine whether the encouraging revenue trends among members are sustained. Investigate what’s driving weak customer retention and test ways to encourage repeat purchases.
4. **Investigate MacBook Air refunds.** Identify the most common reasons customers requested refunds and verify that the 2022 refund records are complete before drawing conclusions.
